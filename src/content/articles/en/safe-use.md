## Why this page exists

If you are reading about emotional or financial abuse, you may not want anyone else to be able to see that. So this page sets out exactly what happens when you visit this site: what is recorded, what is not, and where the limits are of what a website can promise.

## Leaving the site quickly

At the bottom right of every page is the **Leave site** button. It takes you straight to the website of the KNMI (the Dutch national weather service). On a short screen, the button sits at the top of the open menu. It works with a mouse, touch and the Enter key, also without JavaScript. The **Esc key** does not leave the site: Esc only closes an open menu.

With JavaScript, the button replaces the current page in your browser history: the back button then takes you to the page you viewed before this one. Without JavaScript, the back button returns to this page. Pages you visited earlier and other traces of your visit remain. The button does not erase your history and does not close other tabs.

## What the hosting records

This site is hosted on GitHub Pages, a service from GitHub. According to GitHub’s documentation, GitHub Pages records and stores the IP addresses of visitors, for security purposes. An IP address is the address your device or network uses to connect to the internet.

Unheard has no control over that. The site itself does not keep any visitor statistics.

## What this site does and does not store

- **No cookies.** The site itself sets no cookies.
- **No trackers or adverts.** No scripts, fonts or adverts from other parties are loaded. The site’s code contains an option for a visitor counter from Cloudflare, but it is switched off. If that ever changes, it will be stated on this page first.
- **Only your theme choice.** If you click the light or dark button at the bottom right, your browser remembers that choice. This happens in your own browser’s local storage, under the name `ongehoord-theme`. Nothing else is kept, and this choice is not sent anywhere. If you do not click the button, nothing is stored.
- **No forms that send anything.** You cannot send in a story here, create an account or send a message.
- **Searching happens in your browser.** The search function filters a list that is already on the page. What you type is not sent or stored.

## Reflection questions and text fields

Some pages have questions to think about, sometimes with a field where you can type something. What you type there:

- is **not saved** and **not sent**;
- only exists in the tab you have open;
- usually disappears when you close the tab or reload the page. Some browsers restore text when you navigate back to a page, so close the tab if you want to be sure.

Some fields have a button to copy your text. Copied text is then on your device’s clipboard. Other programs can read that clipboard, and some devices keep a clipboard history or share it with other devices. On a shared device, it is wise to keep that in mind.

If you **print** a page, filled-in reflection fields are not printed by default. Printable cards in [Tools](nl/hulpmiddelen/) (in Dutch) are printed with blank lines, so you can fill them in on paper. Only if you tick the box to print your answers yourself will what you typed end up on paper.

## On a shared device

If someone else has access to your phone, computer or accounts, traces of your visit can be left behind:

- **Browser history.** Your browser remembers which pages you visited, including titles such as "What is emotional abuse?".
- **Open tabs and recently closed tabs.** These can often be seen too.
- **Synced accounts.** If your browser is linked to an account, your history can also appear on other devices.
- **Autocomplete.** If you start typing the site’s address, the browser may complete it.

A **private window** (or incognito window) helps prevent some of this. After you close it, the browser usually keeps no history of that window. But a private window does not make you invisible. Your internet provider, your employer or school on their network, and the hosting service can still see your connection. And software watching on the device itself is not stopped by it.

This site cannot erase your browser history for you. There is also no button that promises to. If you are worried that someone is watching, look for information and help on a device you trust if you can, for example a friend’s, at work or in a library.

## Printed paper

A printed page or filled-in card can be found by someone else. Keep paper on which you have written something personal somewhere that feels safe to you, or throw it away when you no longer need it. Also bear in mind that a shared printer sometimes keeps a copy or a print history.

## If you feel unsafe

If you think someone is checking your device, messages or accounts, it is wise to ask for confidential advice first, before you change passwords or settings. A sudden change can, in some situations, cause tension or risk. Veilig Thuis (the Dutch advice and reporting centre for domestic violence and child abuse) gives free and anonymous advice on [0800-2000](tel:08002000). In immediate danger, call [112](tel:112).

There is more about help and asking for advice safely on [Finding help](en/find-help/).
